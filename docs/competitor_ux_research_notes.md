# Competitor UX Research
## Fixora - Competitor UX Research Notes

- **Purpose:** Translate observable public competitor patterns into product-design decisions for Fixora.
- **Research date:** 3 November 2026
- **Important:** This is a directional UX review of public websites and app-store descriptions, not a full hands-on usability study. Features and availability may vary by city and change over time.

## 2. Patterns Worth Learning From

### A. Make service discovery obvious
Competitor pages put service categories and common jobs near the top. Customers often arrive with a task in mind, not a desire to browse a marketplace.
- Keep category cards and a service search close to the top of the home screen.
- Use recognizable labels such as Plumbing, Electrical, AC repair, Appliance repair, and Mobile/device repair.

### B. Price context reduces uncertainty
Expose service prices or offers in discovery experiences.
- Show "From ₹..." on service cards only when a credible demo price is available.
- Separate service charge, convenience fee, tax, discount, and total in the booking review.

### C. Trust claims need operational support
- Use concrete, bounded trust explanations: "Review the estimate before confirming" or "Additional work requires your approval."
- Present technician verification as a clear status with supporting detail.

---

## 3. Recommended Screen-Level Takeaways

| Surface | Pattern to borrow | Fixora interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Categories early | Search + category grid + popular services |
| Service card | Price and service context | Name, starting price, duration, short scope |
| Service detail | Trust and scope | Included work, exclusions, estimate policy |
| Booking | Clear sequence | Address → schedule → issue → estimate → review |
| Booking detail | Status clarity | Timeline, address, amount, support |
| Technician | Job/task focus | Today's jobs, next action, quote state |
| Admin | Operational visibility | Metrics + exceptions + queues + ownership |

---

## 4. What NOT to Copy
- Competitor logos, names, proprietary illustrations, photography, or exact screen layouts.
- Unverified claims such as "best", "guaranteed", or "100% safe".
- Ratings, counts, offers, or customer testimonials without real substantiation.
